    <span class="date">Forthcoming</span>
    <div class="title"><a href="/assets/papers/Moreau_Panon_AEJM.pdf">How Costly Are Cartels?</a></div>
    <div class="meta"><a href="https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/random-author-order" title="Authors listed in certified random order">ⓡ</a> <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/ludovicpanon/research">Ludovic Panon</a> · <em><a href="https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/mac/forthcoming">AEJ: Macroeconomics</a></em> (forthcoming)</div>
    <input type="checkbox" id="abs-cartels" class="abs-toggle">
    <div class="links"><label for="abs-cartels" class="abs-link">Abstract</label> | <a href="https://www.imf.org/-/media/Files/Publications/WP/2022/English/wpiea2022104-print-pdf.ashx">IMF WP</a><a href="https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/aggregate-cost-cartels">VoxEU</a><a href="https://www.lesechos.fr/idees-debats/editos-analyses/vous-avez-dit-cartels-de-crise-1414014">Les Échos (1)</a><a href="https://www.lesechos.fr/idees-debats/editos-analyses/les-cartels-contre-le-pouvoir-dachat-1406542">Les Échos (2)</a></div>
    <div class="abstract">We study the cost of cartels in an oligopoly model with heterogeneous firms, endogenous markups, and collusion. Cartels can amplify or dampen misallocation, by charging supracompetitive markups and reallocating demand towards non-colluding firms. Using French micro data, our calibrated model suggests that cartels operating in the most productive industries may decrease aggregate productivity by up to 0.4% and welfare by up to 0.7%. We also find that standard competitive oligopoly models may understate the cost of markups by about 20%.</div>

  <div class="entry">
    <span class="date">2026</span>
    <div class="title"><a href="/assets/papers/Moreau_Uslu_Merger_Waves.pdf">A Search-Based Theory of Merger Waves</a></div>
    <div class="meta"><a href="https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/random-author-order" title="Authors listed in certified random order">ⓡ</a> <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/semihusluweb/home">Semih Üslü</a> · <em><a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S001429212600098X">European Economic Review</a></em>, July 2026</div>
    <input type="checkbox" id="abs-mergers" class="abs-toggle">
    <div class="links"><label for="abs-mergers" class="abs-link">Abstract</label> | <a href="https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/11/14/a-search-based-theory-of-mergers-and-acquisitions-571856">IMF WP 2025/239</a><a href="https://www.promarket.org/2026/07/20/firm-coordination-creates-unstable-merger-waves/">ProMarket Blog</a></div>
    <div class="abstract">We develop a search-based theory of mergers and acquisitions (M&amp;A) with heterogeneous firms and endogenous search complementarities. We use this model to study how merger incentives, endogenous innovation, and the firm size distribution interact. We derive the law of motion of the firm size distribution, provide closed-form solutions, and solve for firms' investment in internal innovation and external acquisitions. Strategic complementarities in acquisition incentives can generate multiple equilibria, including an unstable equilibrium with high M&amp;A activity. The model therefore offers a new perspective on merger waves as episodes in which favorable fundamentals and coordination interact to generate temporary bursts of acquisition activity. Competition policy can affect both the level of equilibrium merger activity and the set of equilibria the economy can sustain, with implications for innovation, markups, and welfare.</div>
  </div>

  <div class="entry">
    <span class="date">2024</span>
    <div class="title"><a href="/assets/papers/Cahuc_Carcillo_Moreau_Patault_Judge_Bias.pdf">Judge Bias in Labor Courts and Firm Performance</a></div>
    <div class="meta">with <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/pierrecahuc/">Pierre Cahuc</a>, <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/stephanecarcillo/">Stéphane Carcillo</a>, and <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/berengerepatault/home">Bérengère Patault</a> · <em><a href="https://academic.oup.com/jeea/article-abstract/22/3/1319/7258640">Journal of the European Economic Association</a></em>, 2024</div>
    <input type="checkbox" id="abs-judgebias" class="abs-toggle">
    <div class="links"><label for="abs-judgebias" class="abs-link">Abstract</label> | <a href="https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2021/02/12/Judge-Bias-in-Labor-Courts-and-Firm-Performance-50061">IMF WP</a><a href="https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/judge-bias-labour-courts-and-firm-performance">VoxEU</a></div>
    <div class="abstract">This paper documents the existence of judge-specific differences on granting compensation for wrongful dismissal and shows that their consequences are different for small low-performing firms than for other firms. Pro-worker judge bias reduces job creation for all firms, increases the destruction of permanent jobs in small and low-performing firms but reduces it in large high-performing firms. Pro-worker bias reduces employment and survival for small and low-performing firms but has no significant effects on these outcomes for the other firms. The probability that permanent incumbent workers keep their job in firms judged by a pro-worker judge increases in large and high-performing firms, while it decreases in small, poorly performing firms.</div>
  </div>

  <div class="entry">
    <span class="date">2024</span>
    <div class="title"><a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2110701724000611">Diversification in Sight? A Macroeconomic Assessment of Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030</a></div>
    <div class="meta">with <a href="https://www.zamid.co.uk/research">Zamid Alighishiev</a> · <em>International Economics</em>, 2024</div>
    <input type="checkbox" id="abs-saudi" class="abs-toggle">
    <div class="links"><label for="abs-saudi" class="abs-link">Abstract</label></div>
    <div class="abstract">Saudi Arabia, currently the world's largest oil exporter, embarked since 2021 on a large-scale National Investment Strategy (NIS) designed to lift potential GDP growth by diversifying the economy. This article describes the strategy and is the first paper that quantifies its impact on growth using a dynamic general equilibrium model. It also decomposes this impact into two main channels: the direct impact of the investment push and the impact of complementary reforms. We find that the overall strategy — when supported by appropriate fiscal measures, labor supply reform, and higher public sector efficiency — could boost potential non-oil growth by 4.8 percentage points to about 8.8 percent in the medium term. We also assess the growth dividends of a wider range of alternative scenarios around the NIS baseline.</div>
  </div>

    <div class="links"><label for="abs-mortality" class="abs-link">Abstract</label> | <a href="https://www.nber.org/papers/w28193">NBER WP</a><a href="https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2021/02/13/A-Unified-Model-of-Cohort-Mortality-for-Economic-Analysis-50062">IMF WP</a><a href="https://www.imf.org/external/Pubs/FT/irb/2021/springsummer/index.pdf">IMF Research Perspectives</a><a href="https://flavienmoreau.shinyapps.io/app-lmr/">Model</a></div>
    <div class="abstract">We propose a dynamic production function of population health and mortality from birth onward. Our parsimonious model provides an excellent fit for the mortality and survival curves for primate and human populations since 1816. The model sheds light on the dynamics behind many phenomena documented in the literature. Simple extensions of the model can reproduce (1) the existence and evolution of mortality gradients across socioeconomic statuses documented in the literature, (2) non-monotonic dynamic effects of in utero shocks, (3) persistent or scarring effects of wars, and (4) mortality displacement after large temporary shocks, such as extreme weather.</div>

    <span class="date">2026</span>
    <div class="title"><a href="/assets/papers/LAC_Demography_and_LFP_WP.pdf">Offsetting the Demographic Drag? Endogenous Female Labor Supply in Latin American Economies</a></div>
    <div class="meta">with <a href="https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=84O7K3MAAAAJ">Camila Casas</a> and <a href="https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=1xjpsMgAAAAJ&hl=en">Nicolas Gomez Parra</a></div>
    <input type="checkbox" id="abs-lfp" class="abs-toggle">
    <div class="links"><label for="abs-lfp" class="abs-link">Abstract</label> | <a href="https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2026/203/001.2026.issue-203-en.xml">IMF WP</a></div>
    <div class="abstract">The share of Latin America's working-age population has begun to fall, on the back of increased longevity and falling birth rates. The female participation response to falling child dependency is the largest domestic margin available to offset it. We quantify that margin, combining harmonized microdata from seven large economies with a tractable overlapping generations model in which female labor supply responds endogenously to household composition. We first decompose the region's gender participation gap into several margins, with about one third arising from the differential labor supply response to household composition between men and women (a “motherhood penalty”). Embedding these micro-estimates in a general equilibrium framework, we find that the female participation response to falling child dependency can partially offset the demographic drag, raising regional GDP by between 3.1 and 11.3 percent by 2050 relative to a path with participation held fixed. The range reflects both fundamental uncertainty around future paths of comoving national trends and substantial heterogeneous labor supply responses across countries.</div>
  </div>

  <div class="entry">
    <span class="date">Rej.&amp;R</span>
    <div class="title">Beyond Tariffs: Infrastructure Gaps and Trade in Latin America</div>
    <div class="meta">with Rina Bhattacharya, <a href="https://www.rparente.com/">Rafael Parente</a>, and Sam Pienknagura</div>
    <input type="checkbox" id="abs-tariffs" class="abs-toggle">
    <div class="links"><label for="abs-tariffs" class="abs-link">Abstract</label> | <a href="https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2024/12/14/The-Dynamics-of-Trade-Integration-and-Fragmentation-in-LAC-559484">IMF WP</a><a href="https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2023/11/16/how-latin-america-can-use-trade-to-boost-growth">IMF Blog</a><a href="https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/latam-hasnt-done-enough-to-boost-trade-and-reduce-inequality-imfs-georgieva-says/">Bloomberg Línea</a><a href="https://www.economist.com/the-americas/2024/06/13/why-latin-america-is-the-worlds-trade-pipsqueak">The Economist</a></div>
    <div class="abstract">Based on “The Dynamics of Trade Integration and Fragmentation in Latin America” (with Rafael Machado Parente). Trade barriers and poor infrastructure play an important role in limiting trade integration in Latin America and the Caribbean (LAC). Closing half of the infrastructure gap between LAC and advanced economies could lift exports by 30 percent. Reducing import tariffs could boost LAC's trade, but its responsiveness is lower than in other EMDEs, particularly in the long run, due to the region's specialization in agricultural exports with inelastic demand and supply constraints like growing cycles and weather conditions. Amid deepening global trade tensions, LAC is well placed to withstand a mild trade fragmentation scenario, in which trade barriers are erected only among large economies. However, the region's output losses could be sizable in more extreme scenarios, where the global economy splinters into competing economic blocs and LAC loses access to important markets. Boosting trade, including regional trade, could pay a double dividend of lifting growth in the region while mitigating risks from global fragmentation.</div>

    <div class="meta">with <a href="https://www.sophiaychen.com/">Sophia Chen</a>, <a href="https://ryumatsuura.github.io/">Ryu Matsuura</a>, and Joana Pereira</div>
    <input type="checkbox" id="abs-satellite" class="abs-toggle">
    <div class="links"><label for="abs-satellite" class="abs-link">Abstract</label> | <a href="https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2024/04/05/Harnessing-Satellite-Data-to-Improve-Social-Assistance-Targeting-in-the-Eastern-Caribbean-547416">IMF WP</a></div>
    <div class="abstract">Prioritizing populations most in need of social assistance is an important policy decision. In the Eastern Caribbean, social assistance targeting is constrained by limited data and the need for rapid support in times of large economic and natural disaster shocks. We leverage recent advances in machine learning and satellite imagery processing to propose an implementable strategy in the face of these constraints. We show that local well-being can be predicted with high accuracy in the Eastern Caribbean region using satellite data and that such predictions can be used to improve targeting by reducing aggregation bias, better allocating resources across areas, and proxying for information difficult to verify.</div>

  <div class="entry">
    <span class="date">Revising </span>
    <div class="title"><a href="/assets/papers/Moreau_Capital_Labor_Substitution.pdf">Inferring Capital-Labor Substitution from Firm Size Distortions</a></div>
    <input type="checkbox" id="abs-klsub" class="abs-toggle">
    <div class="links"><label for="abs-klsub" class="abs-link">Abstract</label></div>
    <div class="abstract">I propose a novel identification strategy to estimate the elasticity of substitution between capital and labor at the firm level, taking advantage of a set of size-dependent policies. These policies create a break in the size distribution of French firms by employment. To the extent that firms around the threshold distort their capital-labor ratio, the elasticity of substitution can be recovered from firm-level distorted factor ratios and the amount of bunching. On the other hand, because firms have an incentive to under-report their size, part of the apparent distortions is only due to evasion. I derive closed-form formulas that link the elasticity to observed distortions at the firm level. I then aggregate these micro elasticities to analyze capital-labor substitution in the aggregate economy. I find that firm-level distortions are small, and therefore that the micro-elasticities are small and close to 0.1. I obtain an aggregate elasticity of 0.3, about half as small as the values usually assumed. Moreover, the discrepancies between self-reported employment and a measure of employment made by the administration provide direct evidence that evasion accounts for a large fraction of the observed distortions.</div>
  </div>
